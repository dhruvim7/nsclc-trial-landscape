# NSCLC pipeline: what the registry shows

Dhruvi Mewada · September 2026 · Data: ClinicalTrials.gov, 7,194 interventional trials, downloaded 28 September 2026

## Bottom line

Lung cancer drug development is busier than ever, but the growth is no longer coming from PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies. New trial activity has moved to ADCs, KRAS inhibitors, newer EGFR drugs and PD-1-based bispecifics, and it is increasingly run in China and by a wider set of companies. For anyone entering this space, the question is less "is there room?" and more "which mechanism, and where?"

## 1. The field is still growing

Trial starts rose from 173 in 2010 to 462 in 2025. Phase 2 grew fastest (65 to 197 starts a year) and Phase 3 starts roughly tripled (19 to 60). The industry-led share stayed between about 40% and 51% every year, so academic groups grew alongside companies rather than being pushed out.

## 2. More players, less concentration

Comparing 2016-2020 with 2021-2025, the number of companies leading NSCLC trials rose from 290 to 409, and the top five's share of industry trials fell from 21% to 15%. HHI dropped from 144 to 94, far below the 1,500 level usually treated as concentrated. AstraZeneca is still the most active sponsor, but two Chinese companies, Sichuan Baili and Jiangsu Hansoh, are now among the ten most active. No single company controls the field. That leaves room for partnering and licensing deals, but also means more competitors per target.

## 3. Where the activity is moving

| Mechanism class | Trials started 2016-2020 | Trials started 2021-2025 |
|---|---|---|
| PD-1/PD-L1 | 625 | 651 |
| EGFR | 182 | 269 |
| ADC | 27 | 72 |
| KRAS | 5 | 57 |
| PD-1-based bispecific | 2 | 36 |
| Anti-angiogenic | 121 | 93 |
| CTLA-4 | 63 | 20 |

PD-1/PD-L1 is still the largest class but barely grew (+4%). The growth went to newer mechanisms, and these are more industry-driven: 75% of recent ADC trials and 60% of KRAS trials have an industry sponsor, against 46% for PD-1/PD-L1. A company entering today with another PD-1 antibody would be joining the most crowded class. ADCs and KRAS are growing faster, but large companies are already committed there.

## 4. China now hosts more trials than the US

The share of new trials with at least one site in China rose from 11% in 2010 to 52% in 2025, passing the US share in 2022. The US share fell from 49% to 39% over the same period. Across 2021-2025, China had 896 trials and the US 831. This is probably an underestimate for China, since many Chinese trials are registered only on China's own registry (ChiCTR). Multi-country trials stayed at around 23-27% of starts, so the growth is mostly in trials run within China.

## 5. Execution risk

Of 3,988 finished trials, 32% were terminated or withdrawn, falling from 37% in Phase 1 to 26% in Phase 3, with little difference between industry and non-industry sponsors. Where a reason was given, 68% of stops were for operational or business reasons (slow accrual, sponsor decisions, funding, site problems) and only about 11% for efficacy or safety; the remaining 21% could not be classified. This matches Williams et al. (2015), who found that 68% of terminated trials stopped for reasons other than trial data. The pattern differs by sponsor: companies mostly stopped for business decisions (214 trials), academic groups for poor accrual (341 trials). Coming from clinical data operations, I found this the most striking result: a large share of trials end because of recruitment and planning, not the science.

## What this can't tell you

Registry data measures activity, not success. A completed trial can still miss its endpoint, and trial counts are not drug programs, since one drug can appear in dozens of trials. The stop rates here are not probability-of-success figures; those come from program-level databases (BIO/Informa/QLS report about an 8% likelihood of approval from Phase 1 across all diseases).
